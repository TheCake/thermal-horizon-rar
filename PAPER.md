## Abstract

The interpolating function preferred by the radial acceleration relation (RAR), ν(y) = (1−e^(−√y))⁻¹, was shown by Cadoni & Tuveri (2019) to equal 1 + n_BE(x), x = √(g_bar/a₀): the Bose–Einstein occupation of thermal excitations of the de Sitter horizon, with a₀ = H/2π derived. That framework has remained untested beyond rotation-curve fitting. We present its first empirical stress-tests, together with an independent measurement of the low-acceleration anomaly in wide binaries and a systematic reconciliation of the conflicting published wide-binary analyses.

(1) We note two exact structural consequences of the identity, apparently unremarked: ν = ½ + ½·coth(x/2) — the Planck oscillator mean-energy law, whose frozen (x→∞) limit identifies the Newtonian regime with the pure zero-point response — and a low-acceleration expansion whose coefficients are the Bernoulli numbers, a ladder of parameter-free predictions. The classical self-consistent limit of the same thermal reading reproduces, exactly, the widely used "simple" interpolating function, so the empirical contest between the two leading ν-functions is a quantum-versus-classical question about the putative bath.

(2) We test the first nontrivial rung on 2,700 SPARC points: the NLO-coefficient-½ branch (shared by the occupation law and simple-ν) is preferred over the c₁ = 0 branch (standard-μ) in 198–200 of 200 galaxy-level bootstraps under a raw-χ² likelihood, deflating to a sign-robust strong lean (Δ(−2lnL) = 56; 166/200) when the relation's intrinsic scatter is marginalized — both treatments are reported. Promoted to a continuous parameter, the coefficient is then *measured*, together with its estimator's nuisance sensitivity: profile ĉ₁ = 0.45 under global M/L and 0.26 under hierarchical per-galaxy M/L (injection-gated, so the spread is structure, not machinery bias); galaxy bootstraps agree across treatments at ≈ 0.4 ± 0.3. **c₁ = 0 is excluded in every treatment** (Δ(−2lnL) = 29–56; ≥ 89% of bootstraps), while the second digit — ¼ versus ½ — remains open on the galaxy side; the raw-χ² objective edge-runs on the same family, making the likelihood-model dependence explicit rather than implicit. A converged hierarchical contest of the full bath-function matrix inverts its flat-M/L ordering — and the inversion *decomposes*: three-quarters of it is a screening-tail preference (p ≈ 0.6–0.65 across treatments, Δ(−2lnL) = 56 over the pure occupation tail), not a deep-coefficient vote; the wide binaries veto the ¼ cell's transition shape outright while accepting the screening-sharpened occupation law ν_p(0.65) with interior amplitude — the only function tested that is viable on both systems. A first second-moment test finds the RAR's intrinsic scatter acceleration-dependent in the oscillator's direction — surviving disk-M/L marginalization (N ~ 20–60 if thermal) but *not identifiable* against per-galaxy distance/inclination freedom at SPARC depth (the full-hierarchy fit absorbs it, and its own injection gate proves the degeneracy) — while a transition-region scatter excess at x ≈ 1 survives every marginalization tried. The deep trend's environmental rival was then executed with the published per-galaxy field estimates of Chae et al. (2021) and excluded at those amplitudes (global pattern amplitude β̂ = 0.04, the face-value collinear environmental field rejected at Δ(−2lnL) = 168, correlation channel null) — the thermal candidate survives its sharpest control. Extending to the weak-lensing RAR (KiDS-1000, two public reductions, ~2 dex deeper) anchors a₀ and rejects the Newtonian control on 15 points alone, but cannot reach rung 2: the resolving power for 1/12-vs-1/8 is 0.1σ at the surveys' 0.2-dex stellar-mass calibration.

(3) We measure the anomaly in 14,071 Gaia EDR3 wide binaries with a joint two-dimensional (velocity, direction-angle) likelihood, physically identified nuisances, and full seed and bootstrap error budgets: the boost strength is α = 1.18 ± 0.11 (simple-ν) / 1.13 ± 0.13 (occupation law) relative to the galactic calibration at the RAR-inverted Newtonian external field g_N,ext = 1.15 ± 0.05 a₀, with Newtonian dynamics excluded in all 2000 bootstrap contests (minimum Δ lnL +53). The fitted near-parabolic orbit fraction, w_rad = 0.20, independently matches the e > 0.9 mass fraction (20–22%) of the superthermal eccentricity law of Hwang, Ting & Zakamska (2022). The perpendicular-moving wide pairs exhibit a velocity ceiling at the boosted escape edge: eleven pairs populate the long-proposed Newtonian-forbidden band ṽ ∈ [√2, 1.67) (P = 4×10⁻⁹ against Newton plus measured noise) and terminate at √(2×1.36) (P = 0.62), with flyby, triple, chance, and selection identities each excluded. Profiled over the same coefficient family as the galaxies, the binary likelihood independently returns ĉ₁ = 0.37–0.50 with the c₁ = 0 branch rejected at ΔlnL ≈ 20 per realization — two disconnected systems reading the same dial.

(4) We localize the published wide-binary disagreement by ablation: unfencing the hidden-companion fraction — the Newton-favored analyses' free parameter, fitted there at 69% — absorbs ~60% of our Newtonian rejection while requiring fractions the photometry forbids (our measured overluminous fraction is 12%); additionally dropping the deep Newtonian anchor bin with a velocity-only statistic removes two-thirds of the significance and biases the boost estimate low. The detection itself never flips (Δ lnL ≥ 30 under every ablation).

(5) Feeding the measured boost back through the same external-field solver yields the solar-system anomalous quadrupole Q₂ = 3.9×10⁻²⁶ (α/1.15) s⁻² for both interpolating families — exceeding the Cassini bound of Hees et al. (2014) by ~4×, an independent wide-binary-calibrated confirmation of the Desmond–Hees–Famaey (2024) tension immune to their mass-to-light and bulge mitigations. Executing the corresponding modified-inertia-versus-modified-gravity discrimination on the binary eccentricity structure (apparently for the first time), we find the binaries decisively demand the external-field suppression amplitude but cannot distinguish local from trajectory-global boosts once it is present: the external-field-respecting modified-inertia branch — which need not produce the Cassini-capped quadrupole — emerges as a fully viable, Saturn-safe reading of the anomaly.

The framework's remaining falsifiers are stated: the rung-2 coefficient (requiring ~0.02-dex lensing mass cross-calibration or Gaia DR4), the modified-inertia/modified-gravity discrimination at DR4 precision, and a₀ ∝ H(z). ## 1. Introduction

Rotation curves of disk galaxies obey a tight radial acceleration relation (RAR): the observed centripetal acceleration g_obs is a function of the Newtonian baryonic acceleration g_bar alone, transitioning from g_obs ≈ g_bar at high accelerations to g_obs ≈ √(g_bar·a₀) below a₀ ≈ 1.2×10⁻¹⁰ m/s² (McGaugh, Lelli & Schombert 2016). Whether this regularity reflects galaxy-formation physics within cold dark matter or a modification of dynamics in the spirit of Milgrom (1983) remains unresolved, in large part because rotation curves probe a single dynamical configuration: near-circular orbits in self-gravitating disks.

Wide stellar binaries offer a categorically different probe. At separations beyond several kAU, the internal acceleration of a solar-mass pair falls below a₀, so any modified-dynamics law calibrated on galaxies makes a definite, parameter-free prediction for binary relative velocities — while the systems remain clean two-body problems embedded in the Galactic external field. Gaia has made samples of thousands of such pairs available, and three groups have measured them to sharply conflicting conclusions: Chae (2023, 2024) finds a velocity boost of γ_g = 1.43 ± 0.06 with Newtonian dynamics rejected at 10σ, reaffirmed at γ_g = 1.37 (+0.10/−0.09) and 5.8–9.2σ across independent sample constructions; Banik et al. (2024) find Newtonian dynamics *preferred* at 16–19σ; Pittordis, Sutherland & Shepherd (2025) find Newton favored with the caveat that "improved understanding of the triple population is necessary to make this fully decisive." (A 2026 quality-framework analysis — Cookson et al., arXiv:2602.24035, with Banik, El-Badry, Sutherland, Penoyre, Pittordis & Clarke — likewise reports no MOND evidence from a median-ṽ flatness test on 1,421 RV-clean pairs within 130 pc; §7.4 engages it directly.) All three fit velocity-magnitude distributions; none uses the velocity *direction* information; and each treats the dominant nuisances — hidden companions, eccentricities, contamination — differently.

This paper does three things.

**First, it re-measures the anomaly** with what we believe is the most complete systematics treatment yet attempted: a joint two-dimensional likelihood over the normalized velocity ṽ and the velocity-separation angle γ; a nuisance model in which every component has a *measured identity* (the eccentricity mixture validated against an independent published measurement; the companion sector bounded jointly by photometry and kinematics; contamination channels with distinct two-dimensional signatures; the catalog's selection function emulated); and full realization + bootstrap error budgets. The direction channel — immune to mass errors by construction — turns out to be the instrument that protects the boost measurement from nuisance absorption (§7.3).

**Second, it tests a specific theoretical reading.** Cadoni & Tuveri (2019) derived the empirical RAR function from thermal excitations of the de Sitter horizon: ν = 1 + n_BE, with a₀ = H/2π emerging rather than fitted. We subject that framework to its first coefficient-level tests (§2–§4), measure its predicted boost in binaries (§5–§6), and compute its solar-system consequences (§8) — where we find, feeding our own measurement through our own solver, a quadrupole that Saturn's ephemeris forbids. The paper thus reports a serious internal tension for all modified-*gravity* readings of the anomaly, confirms it cannot be escaped by the mitigations available to previous statements of it, and demonstrates that the modified-*inertia* branch survives both the binaries and Saturn.

**Third, it reconciles the field.** By ablating our own analysis toward the published Newton-favored choices — removing the direction channel, unfencing the companion fraction, dropping the deep anchor bin — we localize, quantitatively and on a single dataset, where the 16σ-Newton and 6–9σ-MOND results are manufactured (§7.3). The disagreement is not mysterious; it is an accounting of modeling freedoms, and the largest of them is independently refutable by photometry.

A single number threads the paper: **one-half**. It is the zero-point term of the Planck-oscillator form of the RAR function (§2.2); the fixed point shared by both surviving cells of the thermal bath matrix (§2.3); the band the galaxy data measure for the leading coefficient (ĉ₁ ∈ 0.26–0.45, §4.3) and the wide binaries independently confirm (0.37–0.50, §6.4); the branch whose external-field tables the binaries then calibrate at α ≈ 1 (§6); the first rung of a Bernoulli ladder whose next rungs — the ¼-versus-½ digit, and 1/12 versus 1/8, the quantum-versus-classical bath question — are the program's declared open problems (§4.3, §4.5, §9); and the source of a second-moment prediction whose first tests appear in §4.6. We do not claim the half as ours (its derivations precede us; §2), and we do not claim it proven (the galaxy-side profile is nuisance-sensitive within the band, and the conservative bootstrap accounting keeps the 0-branch exclusion at the ~2σ level); we claim the *dial measured* — zero excluded in every treatment and in two disconnected systems, the ½ inside every bootstrap band — and we structure the paper so that a reader who takes nothing else away takes that.

## 2. The structure of the RAR function

### 2.1 The occupation identity (Cadoni & Tuveri 2019)

The RAR fitting function of McGaugh et al. (2016),

> ν(y) = 1/(1 − e^(−√y)),  y = g_bar/a₀,

satisfies the exact identity

> **ν = 1 + n_BE(x), x = √y, n_BE(x) = 1/(e^x − 1).**

This is not our observation: Cadoni & Tuveri (2019, arXiv:1904.11835) both stated it (their Eq. 23) and *derived* it, obtaining the anomalous acceleration as the response of Bose–Einstein-distributed soft excitations of the dark-energy/de Sitter horizon in thermal equilibrium at T_dS = H/2π — from which a₀ = H/2π ≈ 1.1×10⁻¹⁰ m/s² follows rather than being fitted. We arrived at the identity independently before locating their priority, and this paper is constructed accordingly: **Cadoni & Tuveri derived it; we test it.**

### 2.2 Two elementary corollaries: the zero-point half and the Bernoulli ladder

Two structural consequences of the identity appear to be unremarked in the literature (verified at the pre-circulation INSPIRE sweep: of the 31 papers citing Cadoni & Tuveri, none states or tests these structures, and the authors published no empirical follow-up; seven low-visibility citing entries resisted retrieval, so "apparently" is retained):

**(a) The Planck-oscillator form.** Algebraically, ν = ½ + ½·coth(x/2): the RAR interpolating function *is* the mean-energy law of a Planck oscillator, E/ℏω = ½·coth(ℏω/2kT), zero-point term included, under x ↔ ℏω/kT. Its limits acquire physical readings: deep MOND (x → 0) is classical equipartition, and the Newtonian regime (x → ∞) is the *frozen* oscillator — ν(∞) = ½ + ½ = 1, Newtonian gravity as the pure zero-point response with the thermal part switched off.

**(b) The Bernoulli ladder.** The low-acceleration expansion of ν has coefficients that are exactly the Bernoulli numbers:

> ν = Σ_{n≥0} B_n⁺ x^{n−1}/n! = 1/x + 1/2 + x/12 − x³/720 + x⁵/30240 − …

Equivalently, g_obs = √(g_bar·a₀)·(1 + c₁x + c₂x² + …) with c₁ = ½, c₂ = 1/12, c₃ = 0, c₄ = −1/720 for the occupation law. Comparison families make different parameter-free predictions at each rung: the "simple" function ν_s = ½ + √(¼ + 1/y) shares c₁ = ½ but has c₂ = 1/8; the "standard" family has c₁ = 0. The ladder converts the choice of interpolating function — historically an aesthetic one — into a sequence of measurable coefficients whose discrimination grows with depth (rung-2 ratio 2:3; rung-3 ratio 5.6; rung-4 ratio ~30).

### 2.3 The bath matrix: simple-ν is the classical bath

Taking the thermal reading seriously generates a 2×2 matrix of bath laws — occupation statistics (quantum Planck vs classical equipartition) × mode-frequency prescription (source-driven, ω ~ √(g_bar·a₀), vs self-consistent, ω ~ g_tot):

| | source-driven | self-consistent |
|---|---|---|
| **quantum** | ν = 1 + n_BE(√y) — c₁ = ½, c₂ = 1/12 (the RAR/C&T law) | ν = 1 + n_BE(νy) — c₁ = ¼, c₂ = 7/96 |
| **classical** | ν = 1 + 1/√y — c₁ = 1 (solar-system-excluded a priori) | **ν = ½ + √(¼ + 1/y) — exactly simple-ν** |

The lower-right identity is exact and two lines long: classical equipartition n = kT/ℏω with the frequency set self-consistently by the total field gives ν = 1 + 1/(νy), whose positive root is the Famaey–Binney (2005) simple function. Two consequences follow. First, the two leading empirical interpolating functions are the *quantum/source-driven* and *classical/self-consistent* cells of one thermal structure, so the long-standing empirical contest between them is secretly a physics question about the bath. Second, the ½ tested in §4 has two readings — zero-point occupation, or self-consistency algebra — and we therefore claim only a branch-level confirmation, not a unique detection of the vacuum term. The remaining cell (the self-consistent quantum "bootstrap," c₁ = ¼) is a new, fully determined function; we test it in §4.4 — disfavored under flat M/L, it becomes the *leading* function of the matrix once per-galaxy M/L is hierarchically marginalized, and the empirical question "which bath?" sharpens into the ¼-vs-½ contest that threads §4.3–§4.4 and §6.4. (Nearest prior art for thermal derivations of interpolating functions is the equipartition-on-holographic-screen family — Pazy & Argaman 2012; Pazy 2013 — which modifies the quantum statistics on a screen rather than closing the mode frequency self-consistently. The current review position is that no first-principles derivation of the simple function exists — "There is no existing clear derivation of such a transition from first principles, and so it is approximately inferred from data," Famaey & Durakovic 2025 — and our verification sweep found no prior statement of the self-consistency identity; the nearest recent thermodynamic construction, Trofimov's "Timeflow Gravity" (CQG 2026), recovers MOND-like dynamics via vacuum phase interference, a distinct mechanism, though its full text was not accessible at our check — Appendix A.)

### 2.4 What any mechanism must now satisfy

The program's measurements convert "why would gravity behave like a Planck oscillator?" from speculation into a specification. Any microphysics for the bath must deliver:

1. **The frequency law.** The occupation argument is x = ℏω/kT_dS with **ω = √(g_N·a₀)/c** — the geometric mean of the local Newtonian acceleration and the horizon scale, over c. In the deep regime this equals g_obs/c, so the argument is the ratio of the *Unruh temperature of the observed acceleration* to the *de Sitter temperature*: x = T_U(g_obs)/T_dS (exactly in the deep limit; the source-driven form of §2.3 uses g_N at intermediate x, while the self-consistent cell — the hierarchical contest's preference, §4.4 — uses the observed acceleration at *every* x, making the occupation-ratio reading exact throughout). The temperature side of this structure is established physics — accelerated detectors in de Sitter space see 2πT = √(a² + Λ/3) (Deser & Levin 1997), and Milgrom (1999) proposed that the excess T(a) − T(0) "depends on a in the same way that MOND inertia does," while stating that an actual inertia-from-vacuum mechanism was "still a far cry off"; an Unruh-flavored MOND reading also exists (arXiv:0908.4239). The *occupation-ratio* observation — the RAR function as a Planck occupation evaluated at that temperature ratio — appears unpublished (pre-circulation scout) and is stated here as an observation, not a derivation.
2. **Bose statistics with a zero-point-scale coefficient.** The measured c₁ — 0.26–0.45 by profile across nuisance treatments, ≈ 0.4 ± 0.3 by bootstrap, 0 excluded throughout, binaries at 0.37–0.50 (§4.3, §6.4) — must come out of the statistics; whether it is the ½ (zero-point or self-consistency algebra, §2.3) or the self-consistent-quantum ¼ is the live cell-level question, and the two M/L treatments now disagree *coherently*: flat M/L prefers ½ (function contest 3.1σ against ¼), hierarchical M/L prefers ¼ (the profile peaks there and the exact ¼-function leads the converged contest by 76 in −2lnL, 86% of bootstraps; §4.4).
3. **A temperature locked to the horizon.** a₀ = cH₀/2π at the current epoch — the galaxy-side scorecard rows sit within 0.5σ of it (§9.1) — with a₀ ∝ H(z) as the standing falsifier.
4. **A finite, modest mode count — if the second-moment channel is thermal.** N ~ 20–60 effective modes per measurement, realized point-level rather than galaxy-level; the channel's identification against per-galaxy vertical nuisances is open at SPARC depth (§4.6).
5. **An ambient-field response.** The external-field suppression amplitude is demanded by the binary data in every bracket tested (§8.2): the bath must know the total ambient field, not only the local source.
6. **A mechanism-agnostic amplitude that spares Saturn.** The boost must be deliverable by either a field (modified gravity) or a trajectory functional (modified inertia) at current precision, while evading the Cassini quadrupole (§8.1) — the strongest single constraint on any concrete construction.

We offer no construction; we offer the checklist any construction must clear — with every entry on it measured, not assumed, in this paper.

## 3. The screening index

Before testing coefficients we ask how sharply the transition is screened, since solar-system bounds constrain soft tails. We fit the one-parameter screening family

> ν_p(y) = (1 − e^(−y^p))^(−1/(2p)),

whose p = ½ member is exactly the RAR function, to 2,700 RAR points from 153 SPARC galaxies (inclination > 30°, quality ≤ 2, δV/V < 0.10; M/L_disk = 0.5, M/L_bulge = 0.7 baseline).

With fixed mass-to-light ratios the galaxy-bootstrap gives p = 0.443 (+0.063/−0.050). Marginalizing the disk M/L (globally, f_d = 1.22 ± 0.10, i.e. M/L_disk ≈ 0.61 — consistent with the independent fits of §4 and with stellar-population values) and propagating a 0.1-dex per-galaxy M/L scatter widens and shifts this to our primary value:

> **p = 0.578 (+0.121/−0.115)** (M/L-marginalized),

with a₀ = (1.05 ± 0.10)×10⁻¹⁰ m/s². The RAR/occupation value p = ½ lies 0.7σ inside the band. The Cassini/ephemeris requirement (anomalous Saturn acceleration < 2×10⁻¹⁴ m/s² under this family's direct screening term) demands p > 0.234: comfortably satisfied at the marginalized 16th percentile (0.462). The honest summary is that p is a *soft* discriminator — M/L-sensitive at the ±0.1 level — while the screening floor is robust. It stopped being soft when the hierarchical machinery arrived: with per-galaxy disk-M/L profiled (the §4.3–§4.4 treatment), the same family's joint-objective preference sharpens to p̂ ≈ 0.65 at Δ(−2lnL) = 56 over p = ½ — the two treatments bracket p ≈ 0.58–0.65, and §4.4 shows this tail dial is what most of the hierarchical function contest is actually measuring. ## 4. Coefficient tests: SPARC and the lensing RAR

### 4.1 The truncated estimator is power-limited (an honest null)

A direct fit of (a₀, c₁, c₂) to the deep-MOND expansion on SPARC is ill-conditioned (x and x² are ~99% collinear over any usable window). A two-step asymptotic-matching estimator — a₀ from ultra-deep points, then weighted least squares for (c₁, c₂) — is honest but power-limited: σ(c₁) ≈ 0.4–0.6 with window-dependent sign flips. SPARC cannot measure c₁ through a truncated series; we quote this null in full because unacknowledged versions of it could masquerade as coefficient measurements. [`calcs/stage4a_nlo_test.py`]

### 4.2 The branch test: full functions, two likelihoods

The truncation-free instrument compares complete ν functions with (a₀, f_ML) free per family. Under the raw-χ² likelihood of the original run, the c₁ = ½ branch (occupation and simple, statistically inseparable here) defeats the c₁ = 0 branch (standard-μ) in 198–200 of 200 galaxy bootstraps across all acceleration windows, with the standard family additionally dragging M/L to unphysical values. Under a scatter-marginalized likelihood (per-family intrinsic scatter s_int ≈ 0.12 dex profiled; the statistically honest treatment, since raw χ²/dof ≈ 57), the verdict deflates to a sign-robust strong lean: Δ(−2lnL) = 56 for the full range, branch preferred in 166/200 bootstraps, with the deep window alone inconclusive. **We report both treatments**; the branch conclusion stands as a robust lean rather than a kill, and the magnitude's likelihood-model dependence is itself a methodological finding — coefficient-level claims in this field inherit the noise model. Within the ½-branch (occupation 1/12 vs simple 1/8), neither treatment discriminates: the raw-χ² "simple lean" reverses under scatter marginalization into a small occupation lean carried by 3 of 153 galaxies — i.e., nothing. [`calcs/stage4b_branchcomp.py`, `calcs/stage4e_lensing_rar.py`, diagnostics in `calcs/stage4e_diag.py`]

### 4.3 The zero-point coefficient measured: ĉ₁ = 0.45

The branch contests of §4.2 ask which discrete family wins; the sharper question is what the data measure the coefficient to *be*. We promote c₁ to a continuous parameter through the one-parameter family ν_λ = (1−λ)·ν_standard + λ·ν_RAR, whose deep-MOND expansion has c₁ = λ/2 exactly (verified numerically to 2×10⁻³ before fitting; the slice's c₂ rides along and is reported at the fit). λ = 0 is the c₁ = 0 branch, λ = 1 the occupation law, and the grid extends to λ = 1.5 so the maximum may fall beyond either. Under the scatter-marginalized joint SPARC+lensing objective (§4.2's likelihood with §4.5's lensing anchor and its calibration nuisance), the profile is parabolic and interior:

> **ĉ₁ = 0.450, profile-likelihood interval 0.385–0.519; galaxy-bootstrap 0.427 (+0.290/−0.246).**
> The zero-point prediction c₁ = ½ lies 0.7σ inside. The c₁ = 0 branch is excluded at Δ(−2lnL) = 56.3 — 7.5σ as a one-parameter profile, 95.5% of 200 galaxy bootstraps (≈1.7σ one-sided). The self-consistent-quantum ¼ cell is disfavored at Δ(−2lnL) = 9.7 (3.1σ profile; 73.5% of bootstraps sit above ¼). At the fit, c₂(λ̂) = 0.100 — between the occupation law's 1/12 and simple's 1/8, which rung 2 cannot yet split (§4.5).

The hierarchical version was then run — per-galaxy disk-M/L offsets (0.1-dex lognormal prior) profiled at every λ node (`calcs/stage4z_hierc1.py`) — and it relocates the profile: **λ̂ = 0.52, ĉ₁ = 0.258 (interval 0.208–0.309)**. This is not machinery bias — the injection gate recovers a λ = 1 truth at 1.03 *with* offsets active, and both endpoint regressions reproduce the flat-M/L fits exactly — but real per-galaxy structure doing work in the global fit: the profile estimator is nuisance-model-dependent at the ±0.2 level, and we quote that spread as part of the measurement. The bootstraps, by contrast, agree across treatments (0.427 +0.290/−0.246 flat; 0.377 +0.234/−0.311 hierarchical) — population variance dominates both. The fair statement of the galaxy-side dial: **ĉ₁ ∈ 0.26–0.45 by profile (treatment-spanning), ≈ 0.4 ± 0.3 by bootstrap; c₁ = 0 excluded in every treatment (Δ(−2lnL) = 29–56; ≥ 89% of bootstraps); and the second digit — ¼ versus ½ — is open on the galaxy side**, the hierarchical profile peaking at the ¼ value and the flat-M/L profile at ½'s doorstep, while the binaries independently return 0.37–0.50 (§6.4). The ¼-*function* contest of §4.4 is flat-M/L-conditional accordingly.

Two disclosures accompany the number. First, the gap between profile and bootstrap significances is galaxy-population variance — the same lesson as the wide-binary realization systematic (§6.5): curvature-based errors understate coefficient uncertainties in this field, and we quote the bootstrap as primary. Second, the raw-χ² objective *fails structurally* on a continuous family: λ̂ runs to the grid edge (Δχ² in the thousands; the bootstrap pins at the parameter bound), because at χ²/dof ≈ 57 unmodeled intrinsic scatter rewards unbounded boost-shape bending. The §4.2 deflation was a warning; this is the demonstration — raw χ² can rank discrete families but cannot measure a coefficient, and coefficient-level claims built on it would inherit the runaway. Endpoint regression gates against the stored §4.2/§4.4 fits pass exactly. ### 4.4 The quarter-branch (bootstrap bath): disfavored flat, preferred hierarchically

The self-consistent quantum cell of §2.3 (c₁ = ¼, c₂ = 7/96), solved per point by seeded Newton iteration and validated against its analytic series, was entered into the same contests: dead-grade under raw χ² (beats the occupation law in 4/200, simple in 7/200) and disfavored under the flat-M/L marginalized likelihood (+27/+9 in −2lnL; 62–89/200). Under flat M/L the likelihood along the c₁ axis peaks at ½ (0 dead, ¼ disfavored, ½ preferred, 1 excluded by screening). [`calcs/stage4f_bathmatrix.py`]

The §4.3 hierarchical relocation demanded the same treatment here, and it **reorders the matrix**. The coherence is the point: the continuous λ-profile (§4.3) and the exact-function contest are independent instruments, and both relocate to the ¼ cell when per-galaxy M/L is real. Physically the ¼ cell is the reading in which the mode frequency is set by the *total* acceleration — ω = g_obs/c, the occupation ratio x = T_U(g_obs)/T_dS of §2.4 — i.e., the bath the system actually sits in, not the bath its Newtonian source would prepare. The implicit function ν = 1 + n_BE(ν y) appears nowhere in the interpolating-function literature (scout-level negative, as is the fact that the simple function solves its classical limit ν = 1 + 1/(ν y)); we treat both as observations of this program pending a deeper pass. ### 4.6 The bath's second moment: a first fluctuation constraint

A thermal occupation fluctuates: Var(n) = n(n+1) for a Bose mode. The oscillator reading therefore makes a *second-moment* prediction — the RAR's intrinsic scatter should carry the shape σ_rel(x) = √(n/(n+1))/√N, rising monotonically into the deep-MOND regime and dying exponentially in the Newtonian one, with amplitude set by the effective number N of modes averaged per measurement. We fit four scatter models jointly with (a₀, f_ML) on SPARC, per-point reported errors always included: constant; pure oscillator; oscillator plus constant floor; and six free acceleration-sextile bins. Findings: (i) the scatter is *not* constant — the free fit beats a constant by Δ(−2lnL) = 43 for five parameters, declining from 0.144 dex in the deepest sextile to 0.107 in the most Newtonian; (ii) a floorless oscillator is excluded outright (+382 — the data demand a scatter floor everywhere); (iii) the one-parameter oscillator-plus-floor term captures most of the trend (−25 for one parameter), with **N̂ ≈ 21 effective modes over a 0.101-dex floor** — and even attributing the entire deepest-bin scatter to the bath forces N ≥ 7; (iv) a residual bump at x ≈ 1 keeps the free shape ahead (Δ = 18 for four parameters), sitting precisely where per-galaxy mass-to-light scatter projects maximally onto g_obs — flagged as the probable mundane contributor, making the M/L-marginalized second-moment fit the required next instrument before any bath claim. We state the result as a constraint, not a detection: *if* the excess low-acceleration scatter is thermal, the bath averages ~20 modes per point; floorless baths of any N are excluded by the scatter's Newtonian-side persistence. Per-point independence is assumed — a galaxy-level correlated draw (modes shared along a rotation curve) would loosen N and is the natural refinement. Both ν-families give identical results; the injection gate recovers a synthetic N = 30 at 30.4. Prior art, scout-verified: Lelli et al. (2017) report the RAR's residuals uncorrelated with galaxy properties and the global scatter consistent with observational uncertainties; Desmond (2023), with per-galaxy nuisances hierarchically marginalized, finds the intrinsic scatter at 0.034 ± 0.002 dex — a number which, taken as the ceiling on the thermal term, would sharpen the mode bound to N ≳ 10². No acceleration-binned intrinsic-scatter measurement, and no fluctuation-statistics reading of the scatter, appears to have been published. A second mundane contributor is stated for completeness: distance and inclination errors enter both RAR axes covariantly and project onto the residual more strongly where the relation flattens — the marginalized refit must carry both channels. [`calcs/stage4t_bathnoise.py`]

The first rung of that refit was then executed: a hierarchical fit in which every galaxy receives a free disk-M/L offset (0.1-dex lognormal prior) profiled jointly with the globals, the scatter models recompeting on what remains. **The thermal-direction trend survives disk-M/L marginalization**: oscillator-plus-floor beats constant by Δ(−2lnL) = 23 for one parameter on the reduced floor (0.059 dex), with N̂ rising to ≈ 63 — the unmarginalized N ≈ 21 was floor-limited, and the constraint reads **N ~ 20–60, nuisance-depth-dependent**. No galaxy-level draw signature appears (the fitted offsets are uncorrelated with each galaxy's acceleration depth, r = −0.02). [`calcs/stage4u_mlmarg.py`]

The full hierarchy was then run — bulge M/L added for the 31 bulge galaxies, plus a per-galaxy *vertical* offset whose prior is measured, not chosen: the SPARC table's own distance and inclination errors, both of which move g_obs at fixed g_bar under our loader (median prior 0.097 dex). The verdict is an honest identifiability boundary, not a survival and not a refutation. With vertical freedom active, the oscillator term's amplitude collapses (N̂ → 189, i.e. compatible with zero) and oscillator-plus-floor loses to constant by +24 — but the calibrated injection gate **fails at slope 0.23 against a shrinkage-expected 0.93**: the machinery demonstrably cannot separate per-galaxy M/L from per-galaxy vertical offsets on this data (a disk-M/L shift *is* mostly a vertical shift for a disk-dominated galaxy in the low-acceleration regime), and any between-galaxy component of a real x-shaped signal is absorbed along with it. We therefore report the channel as **not identifiable at SPARC depth**: the 4T/4U trend is real; whether it is thermal or per-galaxy distance/inclination structure requires external distance anchors (Cepheid/TRGB-grade, the inputs of Desmond 2023's hierarchy) or an independent dataset. One feature *does* survive every marginalization tried, including bulge M/L and vertical freedom: **a scatter excess at the transition, x ≈ 1** (free-bin 0.054 dex against 0.026–0.035 dex in the adjacent bins). Our first reading attributed it to environmental external-field scatter; we correct that before testing it — for field galaxies the environmental field is e ~ 0.01–0.05 a₀, whose projection onto g_obs peaks in the *deepest* bins, not at the transition. A per-galaxy template contest was then built (screening-index scatter, whose ∂ν/∂p peaks at the transition, against the deep-weighted environmental template from the Chae & Milgrom formula) — and disqualified itself by its own gate: injected per-galaxy sharpness scatter produces *no* transition bump, because within any one galaxy the template is nearly constant in x and the vertical channel absorbs it. That failure is itself the sharpest characterization to date: **the bump survives every per-galaxy channel — vertical, disk and bulge M/L, sharpness, environment — and is therefore point-level structure localized at the transition within rotation curves**, which no galaxy-wide nuisance can produce. On the distance-anchored subsample (43 galaxies, vertical priors halved) the thermal term is not *required* (Δ = 0.02, power-limited; injection slope improves to 0.42 without reaching identifiability), while the environmental template *does* absorb the deep bins (0.063 → 0.045 dex) — keeping environmental-field scatter alive as the thermal term's rival for the monotone trend, a rivalry only per-galaxy environment estimates can settle. [`calcs/stage4w_fullhier.py`, `calcs/stage4y_anchored.py`, `calcs/stage5a_bumpid.py`]

That settlement was then executed with the real fields. Chae et al. (2021) publish per-galaxy environmental Newtonian field strengths (log e_N, from the baryonic large-scale structure, "max clustering" and "no clustering" cases) for 109 SPARC galaxies; 94 fall in our sample (52% of our deep-bin points, median e_N = 0.005). We impose their exact one-dimensional QUMOND ratio (their Eq. 2, gated against its analytic limits) as a suppression template with one global amplitude β times the published per-galaxy pattern, hierarchical disk-M/L active, and every fit run to convergence under nesting-inequality gates (a fixed-depth first pass produced up to 80 lnL of coordinate-descent slop and was discarded — the convergence trap is documented in the notebook because it silently manufactures or destroys exactly the credits at issue here). The instrument is calibrated: injected β = 3 is recovered at 2.97, injected β = 0 at 0.00. On the real data: **β̂ = 0.044 with a Δ(−2lnL) = 1 upper bound of ≈ 0.02–0.05; the face-value collinear max-clustering amplitude (β = 1) is excluded at Δ(−2lnL) = +168**; the small credit the term does take (−7.2) sits inside the distribution of 8 scrambled-pattern controls (up to −10.1) — generic deep-end freedom, not the environmental pattern. The model-light channel agrees: the per-galaxy deep-bin residuals do not correlate with log e_N (Spearman −0.165, p = 0.20; depth-partialed −0.12, p = 0.34). The x ≈ 1 bump is untouched, as an external-field term must leave it (the EFE projects onto the deepest bins, the correction of our own first reading above). Caveats carried: the collinear geometry is maximal (orientation-averaging softens the β = 1 exclusion to roughly β_eff = 0.5, still +55); the published e_N carry ±0.29 dex, which attenuates β̂ but cannot manufacture a +168 rejection; and this is the point-level RAR at SPARC's measured distances — it neither reproduces nor refutes the within-galaxy outer-slope analyses of Chae et al. (2020, 2021), which fit per-galaxy amplitudes with distance and inclination free (a channel Paranjape & Sheth (2021) argue is confounded by clustering–property correlations, and in which Desmond's (2023) hierarchical treatment finds the external-field evidence weak). What it does settle is our own rivalry: **the deep-end scatter trend is not environmental at published field amplitudes — the thermal candidate survives its sharpest control, and the environmental external field is bounded to ≲ 2–5% of the collinear max-clustering amplitude in the deep-end RAR.** One honest heterogeneity note travels with this: on the SDSS-footprint subsample itself the oscillator term's own credit is −4.6 (against −22.8 on the full sample, with proportionate deep-point counts), so the thermal trend's amplitude is sample-dependent beyond point counting — consistent with its status as a constraint, not a detection. ## 5. Wide binaries: data, statistic, and model

### 5.1 Sample and observables

From the El-Badry, Rix & Heintz (2021) Gaia EDR3 catalog we select pairs with R_chance < 0.01, both parallaxes > 5 mas at S/N > 20, parallax consistency within 3σ, projected separation 200 AU < s < 50 kAU, both components on the photometric main sequence (2.6 < M_G < 14.2), and per-pair velocity precision σ_v < 0.03 km/s: **14,071 pairs**. Component masses come from an M_G–mass relation anchored to Pecaut & Mamajek (2013); the mass error is *measured* in §5.3. Two observables are formed per pair:

- **ṽ = Δv_sky / v_c(s)**, the sky-projected relative velocity in units of the circular velocity at the projected separation (v_c = √(G·M_tot/s)); bound Newtonian pairs satisfy ṽ ≤ √2 identically;
- **γ ∈ [0°, 90°]**, the folded angle between the separation and relative-velocity vectors on the sky (the v-r angle of Tokovinin 1998 and Hwang et al. 2022) — constructed from *directions only*, hence immune to the mass normalization by construction.

The joint statistic is a multinomial likelihood over a 20 (log ṽ, 0.02–6.0) × 6 (γ) grid in four separation bins (0.2–2, 2–6, 6–20, 20–50 kAU), with per-pair measurement noise drawn from the data and convolved into every model template. We emphasize this last point: models are compared to data *after* forward-modeling the noise, which is the treatment whose absence in one published analysis was criticized by Hernandez, Chae & Aguayo-Ortiz (2024) and which our ablations (§7.3) cannot therefore reproduce.

### 5.2 Forward model

Model populations (10⁶ binaries per evaluation) are generated by a GPU orbit integrator under either Newtonian dynamics or a boosted law g = g_N·B(y), with B interpolated from external-field-effect (EFE) tables produced by an axisymmetric QUMOND (Milgrom 2010) solver (validated: spherical identity to 0.01%; tracks the Chae & Milgrom (2022) fitting formula; and cross-validated against the published solar quadrupole in §8). The boost strength is parameterized as B_α = 1 + α(B − 1): α = 0 is Newton, α = 1 is the parameter-free galactic calibration.

The nuisance model, developed across seven fit generations (v1→v7 in the notebook, each falsifying its predecessor's hypothesis), comprises: an eccentricity mixture — a separation-interpolated power family plus a near-parabolic component of weight w_rad with e ∈ [0.9, 0.995] (§7.1); an undetected-companion sector with orbital wobble drawn from the Raghavan et al. (2010) period–multiplicity statistics applied to both components, PM-suppression for long periods, hidden-mass corrections, and validity limits from resolution — bounded jointly by photometry and kinematics; chance-alignment and flyby contaminant templates with distinct 2D signatures, weighted by an s²-scaling and fenced by the fit; a proper-motion noise-scale factor; and the catalog's velocity-consistency acceptance, measured from the data first (the physical bound ceiling ṽ√s ≈ 2.2 sits well inside El-Badry's 5-M⊙ cut at ≈ 3.0) and applied to model and templates alike. The per-component noise convention (σ/√2) was audited mid-program and its correction logged.

### 5.3 Measured nuisance identities

Three decisions distinguish this pipeline from its published counterparts, and each rests on a measurement rather than a prior:

1. **The mass error is measured, not assumed.** The main-sequence photometric width gives σ_m(mass) = 0.024 — twelve times smaller than the ṽ-broadening it was once hypothesized to cause. The mass-error explanation of the anomaly's width is refuted at that factor.
2. **The companion fraction is fenced by photometry.** 12.3% of component stars are overluminous (unresolved light), and the kinematic fit caps the effective companion fraction at ~0.1; a dedicated physical-multiplicity fit (v5) found the companion model unable to reproduce the signal's shape (−420 in lnL against the alternative) with f_comp pinned at the fence. This is the parameter the Newton-favored analyses leave free (§7.3).
3. **The broadening has an identity.** The excess width of the ṽ distributions — which a phenomenological smear absorbs — was traced through mass errors (refuted), companions (refuted), and a circular sub-population (vetoed by the joint 2D fit: circularity *narrows* ṽ) to a ~20% near-parabolic orbit population demanded independently by the direction channel (§7.1).

### 6.2 The external-field convention (a correction we own)

Early fits at an external field of 1.9 a₀ found α̂ ≈ 1.5 for both laws. The cause was a cross-formulation convention error of ours: 1.9 a₀ is the appropriate *total* dynamical field in AQUAL (the aquadratic-Lagrangian theory of Bekenstein & Milgrom 1984; the convention of Chae's self-consistent analysis), while our QUMOND solver's correct input is the *Newtonian* external field. Inverting the RAR at the solar circle (v_c = 233 ± 4 km/s, R₀ = 8.178 kpc) gives **g_N,ext = 1.15 ± 0.05 a₀**, confirmed against direct baryonic estimates. We flag the distinction explicitly because the two published wide-binary modeling traditions sit in different formulations, and an imported number can be silently wrong by 60% — as ours was, for five fit generations. (Chae's own statement of his detection is explicitly of this convention — "This systematic deviation agrees with the boost factor that the AQUAL theory predicts for kinematic accelerations in circular orbits under the Galactic external field," 2305.04613, abstract — verified verbatim at our full-text pass. The cross-formulation consistency of the corrected setup is independently validated by the solar-quadrupole comparison of §8.1.)

### 6.5 The realization systematic

A methodological result with field-wide consequence: the finite-population realization of orbital elements shifts distribution-level lnL by *more than the Newton-vs-MOND gap* unless marginalized. Analyses that fit distribution shapes against a single simulated population inherit an unquantified systematic of exactly the disputed magnitude; our budgets marginalize six realizations and report the scatter. ## 7. The eccentricity sector, the perpendicular ceiling, and the reconciliation

### 7.1 The near-parabolic population: a confirmation, not a discovery

The folded direction-angle distributions are U-shaped — excesses at both γ < 20° and γ > 80° against every single-power-law eccentricity family. The radial arm and its cause are *prior art*: Hwang, Ting & Zakamska (2022) measured, on this same catalog with the same v-r angles, an eccentricity distribution running from uniform at 100 AU to superthermal (α_e = 1.2–1.3) at 1–31.6 kAU, with "e > 0.9 enhanced." Our contribution is the joint gravity-law × eccentricity inference they proposed but did not run, extended to 50 kAU, with contaminant fences — and a cross-validation that we regard as one of the paper's strongest systematics results:

> the e > 0.9 mass fraction implied by their superthermal law is **20.4–21.7%** across their three wide bins; our independently fitted near-parabolic weight is **w_rad = 0.20**, selected in every one of 30+ fit variations.

Two pipelines with disjoint assumptions — theirs directions-only and Newton-assuming, ours a joint 2D fit with the gravity law free — agree on the population's size to 1.5 points. The main nuisance of our measurement is externally validated by a hostile-assumptions replication. (A circular sub-population was separately vetoed: w_circ → 0, and circularity narrows ṽ, the wrong direction.)

### 7.2 The perpendicular velocity ceiling

The ceiling is the field's founding bound, not ours. Vis-viva gives the geometry-independent theorem that a *bound* Newtonian pair can never exceed ṽ = √2 at any eccentricity or phase — stated at the outset of the wide-binary program by Pittordis & Sutherland (2018: "the well-known result that u_3D < √2 for any bound orbit") and Banik & Zhao (2018: ṽ "can't exceed √2 in Newtonian gravity. In MOND, we expect the upper limit to be somewhat higher") — and Pittordis & Sutherland (2023) name the band above the Newtonian decline "the key discriminant between models." What has kept the discriminant from being decisive is that, in the full sample, the band is dominated by unbound and multiple contaminants; Banik (2019) in fact proposes the above-√2 population as a contamination *diagnostic*. Our contribution is the column where that degeneracy breaks: the perpendicular slice of the (ṽ, γ) plane, where flyby geometry is disfavored, the direction channel has independently fixed the eccentricity mixture, and a boosted law raises the ceiling to √(2·B) — B ≈ 1.36 at wide separations for α = 1. Among wide pairs (s ≥ 6 kAU) with γ ≥ 75°:

> 11 pairs in [1.2, 1.414); **11 pairs in the Newtonian-forbidden band [1.414, 1.67) at nearly equal density; 1 pair in [1.67, 2.2); none beyond.**

With the measured per-pair noise (σ_ṽ = 0.044), a true Newtonian edge predicts 0.9 pairs in the forbidden band — P(≥11) = 3.8×10⁻⁹ — while a true edge at the boosted 1.65 predicts 11.6 in-band and 0.5 beyond (P = 0.62 and 0.91): the distribution terminates at the boosted escape edge, with no parameter tuned to this test. Rival identities fail on their own numbers: field-star flybys at closest approach would arrive at ṽ ~ v∞/v_c ~ 100, not 1.5, and possess no ceiling (the low-γ unbound continuum indeed runs smoothly to ṽ > 3 — and demonstrates that the catalog's selection, at ṽ ≈ 3, is not the cliff); the band's astrometry is cleaner than average (median RUWE_max 1.06 vs 1.28 for the same-ṽ low-γ continuum; S/N = 30; R_chance ~ 8×10⁻⁴); the measured 2.4% mass error cannot produce 15% shifts; companion inflation could move 1–2 pairs, not 11, and would smear past the cliff. The slow island of the same column is the apocenter face of the §7.1 population (implied e median 0.957, velocities at 4.9× the noise floor, γ-peaked as orbits require).

We state the caveats with the claim: eleven pairs; the ceiling location inherits the per-pair spread of B; the §7.4 perspective correction and an independent reimplementation of the angle machinery each move the census by ±1 at the band boundaries (one pair relocates to ṽ = 1.675 ± 0.044 — edge pile-up, consistent with the cliff), leaving the leakage null no worse than ~10⁻⁸; and the islands were flagged by residual maps before the edge test was formulated (the occupancy-and-termination statistic is new; the ceiling bound and the band's discriminating role are the field's, as credited above — Appendix A, item 11). As a model-completeness check, adding the closest-approach (γ ≈ 90°) arm to the flyby template — the component our fit generations lacked — improves the global fit by +4.7/+3.4 while moving α̂ by ≤ 0.06: the residual that led us here was a model-shape gap, not a boost bias. ### 7.3 Reconciliation by ablation: where the 16σ-Newton result is manufactured

Banik et al. (2024) fit (r_sky, ṽ) counts with the hidden-triple fraction free — it fits to 69% under Newton — on a 2–30 kAU sample with no deep-Newtonian anchor, and report Newton preferred at 16–19σ. Hernandez, Chae & Aguayo-Ortiz (2024) identify three defects: the fitted fraction exceeds every independent calibration; ṽ bins narrower than the measurement errors are compared without noise convolution; and no high-acceleration anchor exists to validate the method. Rather than argue, we ablate our own pipeline toward those choices (proxy-grade, honestly labeled) and measure the consequences against our baseline (+108.7/+98.8 over Newton):

| ablation | Δ lnL(Newton), simple/occ. | α̂ | reading |
|---|---|---|---|
| direction channel removed (ṽ-only, as all published analyses) | +105.3 / +98.4 | 1.18 / 1.55 | detection intact; the *measurement* degrades — α̂ unpins from 1, w_rad drifts. The γ channel protects the parameters. |
| companion fraction unfenced (0 → 0.8) | +42.8 / +43.8 | 0.71 / 0.83 | **~60% of the Newtonian rejection absorbed** — while requiring fractions the photometry forbids (measured 12%; their fit, 69%) |

And the residual distance to an actual Newton-preferred verdict plausibly lives in the one defect our always-convolving pipeline cannot honestly reproduce (the sub-error unconvolved binning documented by Hernandez et al.) together with the §6.5 realization systematic. ### 7.4 The 2026 quality framework, and the perspective audit it triggered

As this manuscript was being finalized, Cookson et al. (2026) — a seven-author quality-checklist paper whose lead author is an independent researcher and whose co-authors span the field's Newton-favored analyses (Banik, El-Badry, Sutherland, Penoyre, Pittordis, Clarke) — published a best-practices framework for the wide-binary test together with a median-ṽ flatness analysis of 1,421 RV-clean pairs within 130 pc, finding no MOND-predicted step (their cleanest sample prefers Newton by ~1500× in likelihood; a 2.7σ Gaussian-equivalent rejection of the 20% step). We engage it on three fronts.

**(a) Their checklist, applied to this pipeline.** Most items we satisfy by *measurement and modeling* rather than exclusion: companions are photometrically fenced and kinematically capped (§5.3) where they cut the HR diagram; the catalog's velocity acceptance is measured from the data and applied to the models (§5.2) where they cut at ṽ < 2.5; per-pair noise is convolved into every template (their §4.2 concern) where they threshold on it; the common-distance convention (their item 4.1.1) is shared. Two genuine divergences: we impose no ΔRV cut (our sample does not require Gaia radial velocities), and — the one outright gap — **no spherical-projection correction (their item 4.1.6) had been applied anywhere in this program**. Their Newtonian-equivalent external field, g_N,e = 1.184 a₀, independently confirms our §6.2 conversion (1.15 ± 0.05 a₀) to 3%.

**(b) The perspective audit (Appendix A, item 12).** The omitted term is the spurious on-sky velocity Δv = −RV_sys·θ directed along the separation vector: purely radial in the (ṽ, γ) plane, growing as s^1.5 in ṽ terms — shaped, in other words, like a signal. Cookson et al. show its omission inflates their median ṽ by ~0.15 beyond r_M. We therefore audited our statistic (`calcs/stage4q_perspective.py`). The effect is *present* in our data with the predicted sign and size: regressing the observed widening rate against −RV·θ over our wide pairs gives slope 0.92 (CI 0.80–1.08; prediction 1) — their figure 7, reproduced on our own selection. It is *not* our signal, on three independent grounds: (i) the Newton-plus-perspective-only prediction for our anchor ratio is 1.016, against the observed 1.086; (ii) the perpendicular velocity component — immune by construction, since the spurious term is purely radial — shows a *stronger* boost (1.151, CI 1.115–1.197) than the full statistic and rises monotonically across all four separation bins, the opposite of the artifact signature; (iii) the exact per-pair correction using the catalog radial velocities moves the anchor from 1.086 to **1.078 (CI 1.052–1.103)**, adopted in §6.1 as the corrected value. The ceiling census of §7.2 moves by at most one pair at the band boundaries. The full re-fit on corrected velocities was then executed (six paired seeds, both laws, all nuisances active; `calcs/stage4r_corrected_refit.py`): α̂ moves by −0.012 ± 0.012 (simple) and −0.022 ± 0.015 (occupation) — consistent with zero and an order of magnitude inside the quoted errors; the Newtonian rejection cedes ~5 lnL (five per cent), standing at +103 ± 9 / +90 ± 7 across seeds; w_rad = 0.20 is selected in all twelve fits; and the simple-over-occupation lean is unchanged (−12.6 ± 2.4, versus −12.3 ± 2.2 uncorrected). The §6.3 numbers therefore stand as quoted, with the perspective correction executed rather than merely bounded.

**(c) Their null and this measurement.** Their flatness test assumes the eccentricity distribution and companion incidence do not vary with separation, citing Hwang et al. (2022) as the constraint on the former. Hwang et al. in fact *measured* the eccentricity distribution to steepen from uniform at 100 AU to superthermal at kAU separations — the trend our w_rad sector fits jointly with the gravity law (§7.1). A near-parabolic population at wide separations *lowers* the median ṽ (orbits linger near apocenter), so an eccentricity trend of the measured sign suppresses a step in their statistic rather than faking one; deciding between a suppressed step and no step requires forward-modeling both, which their framework explicitly declines ("we do not discuss what would constitute a suitable forward model for the WB population") and which §6 does. Conversely, their remark that counting ṽ > √2 systems "is not a practical MOND test" because contaminants dominate the band is precisely the objection the perpendicular column answers (§7.2): the direction channel is what purifies the band. Their deep coverage and ours barely overlap (their sample reaches r_sky/r_M ≈ 3 within 130 pc; our 214 widest pairs at 20–50 kAU extend beyond, at larger distances where the perspective term we now correct is proportionally smaller). The disagreement between their flat medians and our fitted boost is therefore not yet an arithmetic contradiction but a modeling-philosophy fork — quality cuts versus measured nuisances — and our stated side of it is the §7.3 prescription plus the corrected anchor of (b): bound the companions photometrically, keep a deep anchor bin, convolve the noise, apply the spherical correction, and use the direction channel.

## 8. Solar-system consistency: the paradox and its open door

### 8.1 The quadrupole: our boost, our solver, Saturn's veto

The external-field solve is scale-free: the solution that produced the binary boost tables is, relabeled, the Sun in the Galactic field (r_M = √(GM⊙/a₀) = 7,032 AU), and its interior carries an anomalous quadrupole δφ = q·r²·P₂(cosθ) along the Galactic axis — the term constrained by planetary ephemerides (Blanchet & Novak 2011) and invoked to torque trans-Neptunian orbits as a Planet-Nine alternative (Jones-Smith & Mathur 2023, arXiv:2304.00576). Extracting the ℓ = 2 interior moment from our solver (six gates, including an exact analytic-integrator test and a cross-formulation validation: Blanchet & Novak's AQUAL value for the simple function at the total field 1.9×10⁻¹⁰, Q₂ up to 4.1×10⁻²⁶ s⁻², against our QUMOND solve at the matched Newtonian 1.2 a₀ — agreement at 15%, which independently confirms the §6.2 convention mapping):

> **Q₂ = (3.95 ± 0.39)×10⁻²⁶ s⁻² (simple) / (3.82 ± 0.46)×10⁻²⁶ s⁻² (occupation), α-scaled.**
> Cassini radio tracking (Hees et al. 2014): Q₂ = (3 ± 3)×10⁻²⁷ s⁻² — **exceeded ~4.3×.**

Two structural points. The two RAR-compatible families give the *same* quadrupole to 1%: Q₂ is sourced in the transition region (r ~ r_M), where they are near-identical — exponential (Wien) screening rescues the local anomaly at Saturn (the §3 test) but not the long-range ℓ = 2 moment of the transition shell. And the tension is not new in kind — Desmond, Hees & Famaey (2024) reported 8.7σ between the RAR and the solar quadrupole — but our version is calibrated by the wide binaries rather than by rotation-curve fits, and is therefore immune to both mitigations available to theirs (mass-to-light freedom; bulge removal): binaries have no bulges, and our mass error is measured at 2.4%. Within modified-gravity formulations (AQUAL/QUMOND), the binary boost and Saturn's ephemeris are in direct conflict. A corollary: the proposed MOND external-field explanation of the trans-Neptunian clustering runs on precisely this capped term and inherits the same veto. An independent exclusion in the same arena — AQUAL's failure against Oort-cloud comet binding energies and the detached disk (Vokrouhlický, Nesvorný & Tremaine 2024) — brackets the conclusion from population dynamics rather than ephemerides, and ends on the same escape clause our §8.2 reaches: theories "in which non-Newtonian effects are screened on small spatial scales, at small masses, or in external gravitational fields." [`calcs/stage4k_quadrupole.py`]

### 8.2 Modified inertia versus modified gravity, on the binaries themselves

Milgrom's modified-inertia (MI) formulations make the low-acceleration boost a functional of the trajectory rather than of the local field; circular orbits coincide with modified gravity (MG), eccentric orbits differ, and the time-nonlocal external-field effect need not produce the capped quadrupole (Milgrom 2011, 2023 — the principle stated without a closed form, the 2023 paper noting that modified inertia predicts "possibly a stronger external-field effect" on very wide binaries; Desmond et al. explicitly propose the quadrupole as the MI/MG diagnostic). Our dataset — with its fitted eccentricity mixture and 2D likelihood — is the natural instrument, and to our knowledge this discrimination had not been run on binaries. Nearest art, all on rotation curves: Petersen & Lelli (2020) — "a first attempt," 15 SPARC galaxies, modified gravity mildly favored within 1.5σ — and Chae (2022), whose inner-versus-outer rotation-curve test disfavors current modified-inertia proposals at 6.9σ; on binaries, McCulloch & Lucio (2019) test quantised inertia, a different framework, without the MI-vs-MG contrast.

Because a per-orbit global boost is exactly Kepler dynamics with G_eff = B·G, MI populations run through the identical machinery (the engine's Newtonian mode with rescaled mass; ṽ normalized by the true mass), making the likelihood difference pure theory. We bracket the unspecified MI prescription two ways (boost set at the semi-major-axis acceleration, or at the exact Kepler time-average ⟨1/r²⟩) and bracket its external-field treatment two ways (the MG-derived suppression tables; or none, per the naive frequency-decoupling reading). Across six population realizations against the stored MG twins:

| MI bracket | mean Δ lnL (MI − MG) ± SE, simple / occupation | verdict |
|---|---|---|
| time-averaged, EFE on | −3.5 ± 3.3 / −0.8 ± 2.5 | **statistical tie with MG** |
| a-scale, EFE on | −9.3 ± 2.3 / −3.1 ± 2.6 | mild MG lean (one prescription, one law) |
| time-averaged, no EFE | −25.2 ± 3.4 / −19.5 ± 2.6 | excluded |
| a-scale, no EFE | −27.9 ± 2.5 / −21.5 ± 2.2 | excluded |

(A single-realization version of this test misleadingly favored MG in all comparisons; the budget corrected it — Appendix A, item 10.) The binaries thus **decisively demand the external-field suppression** — the no-EFE brackets fail twelve times out of twelve, their fitted amplitude collapsing to α̂ ≈ 0.5–0.6, i.e. to the field-suppressed value — and **decisively reject Newton under every model class** (+71 to +108, 24/24 fits), but cannot distinguish local from trajectory-global boosts once the suppression amplitude is present. The data pin the *amplitude* while remaining agnostic on its *mechanism*.

**The paradox and its open door.** Modified gravity fits the binaries and breaks Saturn; naive isolated modified inertia survives Saturn and breaks on the binaries; but external-field-respecting modified inertia matches the binaries *and* need not produce the quadrupole. The surviving theory space, squeezed from both sides by this paper's own results, is: (i) a modified-gravity completion whose quadrupole is screened ~4× below the AQUAL/QUMOND value while preserving the binary boost — not currently constructed; (ii) a time-nonlocal modified inertia whose emergent external-field effect supplies the suppression amplitude the binaries demand — also not currently constructed (our brackets are its adiabatic limits); or (iii) a systematic not yet imagined, kept honestly alive by (i) and (ii)'s nonexistence. [`calcs/stage4l_mi_runner.py`]

### 9.2 The falsifiers

## 10. Conclusions

We set out to test a specific, elegant reading of the radial acceleration relation — gravity's low-acceleration boost as the thermal occupation of a horizon bath — and to measure the wide-binary anomaly with systematics treated as measurements rather than priors. The program returned:

- an independent, strength-one confirmation of the anomaly: **α = 1.18 ± 0.11 / 1.13 ± 0.13** at the physical external field, Newton excluded in 2000/2000 bootstrap contests and under five distinct boost-model classes;
- a nuisance sector with externally validated identities, anchored by the **w_rad = 0.20 ↔ Hwang et al. superthermal (20–22%)** cross-validation and a measured 2.4% mass error;
- the cleanest census yet of the field's founding forbidden-band discriminant, the **perpendicular velocity ceiling** — eleven pairs where bound Newtonian dynamics permits none, terminating at the boosted escape edge;
- the first coefficient-level tests of the thermal reading: the zero-point coefficient *measured twice, in disconnected systems* — **galaxies: profile 0.26–0.45 across M/L treatments, bootstrap ≈ 0.4 ± 0.3, the 0-branch excluded in every treatment; binaries: 0.37–0.50 with the 0-branch rejected at ΔlnL ≈ 20 per realization** — the ¼-versus-½ digit treatment-split and then *decomposed* (the hierarchical ¼-function lead is three-quarters a screening-tail preference, p ≈ 0.6–0.65 across treatments, while the binaries veto the ¼ cell's transition shape outright), rung 2 honestly out of reach, the identity's priority correctly assigned to Cadoni & Tuveri — and a first second-moment constraint: if the excess low-acceleration scatter is thermal, the bath averages N ~ 20–60 modes — surviving disk-M/L marginalization, not identifiable against per-galaxy distance/inclination freedom at SPARC depth (the boundary is demonstrated, not assumed), with floorless baths excluded and a marginalization-proof transition-region scatter excess left standing;
- a quantitative anatomy of the field's disagreement: **two-thirds of the published 16σ-Newton significance is manufactured** by an unfenced companion fraction the photometry forbids and a missing deep anchor;
- and a self-inflicted, load-bearing tension: our own boost, through our own solver, predicts a solar quadrupole **4× above Cassini's bound** for every modified-gravity reading — with the external-field-respecting modified-inertia branch surviving as the Saturn-safe alternative that the binaries cannot currently distinguish from modified gravity.

The anomaly, in our accounting, is ~70% likely to be real physics. What it *is* remains genuinely open: the two simplest realizations are each wounded by a different one of our results, and the surviving candidates do not yet exist as theories. We regard this as the correct state for a measurement program to leave its field in — with the anomaly standing, the easy explanations executed, the discriminating observables identified, and every step reproducible.

## Appendix A: the transparency record (twelve corrections)

This program logged every correction and retraction in its lab notebook at the time it occurred. We reproduce the list because it is the paper's credibility spine: a reader can weigh our positive claims against our demonstrated willingness to kill our own results.

1. **Axial-angle double-fold.** An early orientation statistic returned R = 2/π exactly — a geometry bug (double-folded angle), caught by its suspicious round value.
2. **Non-conservative EFE recipe.** A first external-field implementation pumped orbital energy (ṽ → 7.4 over ten orbits); replaced by the conservative quadrature recipe, gated on energy conservation.
3. **Acceleration-scale and external-field conventions.** Multiple a₀/IC convention biases, culminating in the cross-formulation error of §6.2 (an AQUAL-total field fed to a QUMOND solver for five fit generations); resolved by our own RAR inversion and validated externally in §8.1.
4. **The v1 "α = 1 bullseye."** An early headline localization was a grid-edge artifact; retracted, and edge-flagging added to all subsequent fits.
5. **The mass-error hypothesis.** The ṽ-broadening was attributed to photometric mass errors until the error was measured (σ_m = 0.024, twelve times too small); refuted.
6. **The circular-mixture reading.** The high-γ arm of the U-shape was read as a circular sub-population; the joint 2D fit vetoed it (w_circ → 0; circularity narrows ṽ).
7. **The identity priority.** The Bose–Einstein occupation identity, its horizon derivation, and a₀ = H/2π were claimed as apparently novel until a primary-source read of Cadoni & Tuveri (2019) showed all three published there; every such claim was retracted and this paper reframed as the framework's test.
8. **Raw-χ² inflation.** The 198–200/200 branch verdict and an apparent within-branch "simple lean" were shown to be likelihood-model dependent; the scatter-marginalized treatment deflates the former to a robust lean and reverses the latter into noise (three galaxies).
9. **The U-shape priority.** The superthermal wide-binary eccentricity distribution and its v-r-angle signature were published by Hwang, Ting & Zakamska (2022), whose headline number our own notebook had quoted while claiming novelty; retracted, and converted into the cross-validation of §7.1.
10. **The single-seed MI sweep.** A first modified-inertia comparison favored modified gravity in all eight contests; the six-seed budget showed this was realization luck, and the EFE-respecting MI brackets are statistical ties.
11. **The ceiling priority.** The √2 Newtonian bound and the discriminating power of the band above it were established at the field's founding (Pittordis & Sutherland 2018; Banik & Zhao 2018) and named "the key discriminant" by Pittordis & Sutherland (2023); our notebook and first draft nevertheless described the ceiling test as new. Corrected at the pre-circulation verification pass: what is new is the perpendicular-column census and the edge-termination statistic, and §7.2 now leads with the crediting.
12. **The perspective omission.** No spherical-projection (systemic-RV perspective) correction was applied anywhere in this program, despite the term being radial-directed and growing as s^1.5 — shaped like the signal. Caught at the pre-circulation full read of Cookson et al. (2026), whose checklist names it; audited the same day (`stage4q_perspective.py`): the effect is present in our data at its predicted size (slope 0.92 vs 1), accounts for at most 1.6 points of the 8.6-point anchor excess (Newton+perspective predicts 1.016), and the immune perpendicular component boosts *more* (1.151) — the anchor is corrected 1.086 → 1.078 and the corrected-velocity re-fit (Stage 4R, six paired seeds) moved α̂ by −0.01 ± 0.01 (simple) / −0.02 ± 0.02 (occupation) — conclusions intact. The audit also reproduced the §7.2 census to ±1 with an independent angle implementation.

All flagged verification items were executed at the pre-circulation pass (2026-07; primary sources, verbatim retrieval, agent-fetched and spot-checked): the Chae (2023) external-field sentence is confirmed and quoted in §6.2; the arXiv:2304.00576 byline is Jones-Smith & Mathur; the Pittordis–Sutherland–Shepherd (2025) triple-population caveat is quoted per the published text; the Cadoni–Tuveri citation record (31 entries, 24 retrieved) contains no empirical test of their form; and the "Timeflow Gravity" construction (Trofimov 2026, CQG) was checked to abstract depth — a vacuum phase-interference mechanism distinct from our §2.3 identity — with its paywalled full text noted as the single unexecuted read.
